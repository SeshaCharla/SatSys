## Overall Evaluation
This paper presents a novel approach for modeling and
parameter estimation under catalyst saturation in a diesel
engine SCR-ASC aftertreatment system to achieve onboard
catalyst aging diagnostics. By utilizing the response under
saturation as a system upper bound constraint, the study
cleverly addresses the unidirectional bias uncertainty
caused by the $\text{NO}_x$ sensor's cross-sensitivity to
ammonia. The authors provide thorough validation using both
engine test-cell data and real-world heavy-duty truck road
driving data, demonstrating that the proposed algorithm
holds significant potential for practical engineering
applications. The paper is well-structured, tightly
integrating theoretical derivation with engineering
validation.
## Detailed Comments
1. Formatting of Chemical Compounds: $\text{NO}_x$,
$\text{NH}_3$, $\text{O}_2$, and $\text{N}_2$ must be
strictly rendered in roman font.
2. Engineering Feasibility and Necessary Conditions: The
authors state that a necessary condition for the proposed
parameter estimation and aging detection framework is that
the data must contain a "sufficiently long" or
"sufficiently many" samples close to catalyst saturation.
However, during real-world heavy-duty truck operations, the
vehicle may encounter prolonged light-load cruising,
extended downhill driving, or specific conservative driving
habits, which might result in entire driving segments
failing to trigger catalyst saturation at all.
Under such scenarios, will the system generate false alarms
for catalyst aging?
In a practical onboard OBD strategy, how long is the system
expected to wait, or under what specific operating
conditions can it ensure this necessary condition is
satisfied?
It is recommended that a supplementary description or
discussion regarding this be added to the main text.
3. Interpretation of Threshold $\beta$: In the test-cell
validation, the decision thresholds are set to
$\beta_{\text{hFTP}} = 4.00$ and $\beta_{\text{RMC}} =
10.00$, respectively. Why is the threshold abruptly scaled
up directly to $\beta_{\text{truck}} = 250$ in the
real-world truck driving segment analysis?

This paper presents a practical to diagnosing catalyst
aging in diesel Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR)
systems. It proposes a model that explicitly accounts for
the interplay between a gas parcel's residence time and the
sensor's sampling frequency.
The proposed works suffers from the limitation that the
parameter estimation algorithm fundamentally requires the
data to contain segments where the catalyst is fully
saturated. As the authors acknowledge, if the catalyst does
not reach saturation, the algorithm incorrectly fits the
maximum observed surface coverage as the absolute capacity,
which would entirely skew the aging diagnostics. This is a
severe bottleneck for real-world deployment, evidenced by
the fact that the actual truck drive segments evaluated in
the study featured very few samples (often under 10%) near
saturation.
Furthermore, the authors note: "The saturated catalyst
model’s response does not depend on the previous state but
only on the inputs, making the response independent of the
initial conditions." This effectively means (at least to my
understanding) that the dynamics are irrelevant, and that
is the reason a simple MLE point estimator suffices. In
that sense, this paper does not fit the scope of MECC,
because the system dynamics don't really matter.
